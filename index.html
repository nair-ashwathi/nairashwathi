I am a final-year PhD researcher at Swinburne University of Technology and a member of OzGrav, where I study how gravitational-wave sources form and evolve across different astrophysical scenarios. My work uses stellar evolution models to compare theoretical predictions with observations from radio and gravitational-wave astronomy, with a focus on double neutron stars and black hole-neutron star systems.

Outside research, I am deeply interested in art, observation, and science communication. I enjoy documenting everyday experiences through creative practice and learning across disciplines.

Community

Teaching, outreach, and equity work that I care deeply about.

Teaching

Teaching little curious minds gives me so much joy. I have been a high school physics and maths lecturer for several years, and I continue to enjoy helping students build confidence in difficult concepts. As part of my PhD journey, I have also supervised short-term work experience students and supported early-career learners through hands-on mentoring.

Outreach

Outreach is one of the most meaningful parts of my work. I enjoy creating spaces where science feels welcoming, approachable, and exciting for broader audiences. Through public engagement and communication, I hope to make astrophysics more visible and more inclusive to the people who might one day become part of it.

Equity, Diversity & Inclusion

I am currently a student representative on the Equity, Diversity & Inclusion committee at the Centre for Astrophysics and Supercomputing. This work matters to me because I believe that strong research communities are built through care, representation, and access.
